# Role-model demo videos, frame-level review (2026-10-17)

Method and limits: YouTube playback would not start in the automated Chrome (player stuck at state -1, no audio), so I could NOT hear any video. Instead I pulled YouTube's own storyboard sprite sheets (320x180 thumbnails, one every 2 s; ConfidFit every 1 s) and viewed frames at 0-16 s, the middle, and the last ~10 s. Durations and descriptions come from the player metadata. Voice is judged from burned-in captions, descriptions and the ASR caption-track flag only; every voice verdict is "unverified by ear".

## 1. Aloud (1st place) - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rPntXmDqHQ (Devpost: https://devpost.com/software/aloud-lxqr70) - checked 2026-10-17
- (a) Length: 2:55 (175 s).
- (b) First 10 s: 0 s black, then a dark title scene "Two bottles. Same size. Same cap. Same weight." followed by the hook line "If you cannot see the label, the shelf goes silent." Hook is a story/problem, not a logo. By 10-14 s a 3-card stat slide appears: 8.5M blind or low-vision Americans / Sued (Sephora ADA suit) / 5,114 ADA digital-accessibility lawsuits. No intro logo, no "Hi I'm...".
- (c) Voice: a narration track is implied. Every spoken line is burned in as a bottom caption (word-timed, e.g. "Eight and a half million Americans are blind or have..."), and YouTube generated an English auto-caption track, which means speech exists. Human vs synthetic: unverified (could not hear). The clean, evenly paced captions suggest scripted voiceover.
- (d) App shown: a Pixel-style phone frame (status bar, red recording chip) with real app screens, placed in the right third of a dark 16:9 slide; the left two-thirds carry big serif headline text ("A selfie, framed by sound alone.", "Honesty is engineered, not hoped for.", "Verify your look.") plus small mono "chips" and a stat. Chapter structure = one headline per feature (Scan a product, Talk with Aloud, Selfie, Verify your look). Includes a real person taking the selfie (face visible at 84 s). At 145 s a "Wired end to end, not wrapped" slide with the real API calls (POST/GET lines). 150-160 s the Android emulator is shown. Headlines are large (about 1/12 of frame height); in-phone text is tiny and mostly unreadable at thumbnail size, so the headline + caption carry the meaning.
- (e) Close: from about 164 s to 175 s (about 10 s) a still end card: "Beauty, aloud. The first beauty AI a blind shopper can use alone." with THREE QR codes (Live web app / TestFlight / Android APK) and two text URLs (project site + github.com/StephenSookra/aloud). Longest and most actionable end card of the five.

## What SkinProof's video should copy
1. Open on a concrete story/problem line over visuals within 2 s, not a logo: Aloud's "Two bottles... the shelf goes silent." and ShadeSpan's "The same t-shirt. Two customers." beat ToneGrid's silent 16 s of static page.
2. Burned-in word-timed captions plus one big headline per scene (Aloud) so the video works muted and the message survives small UI text; put the app in a device frame at the side, not full-width raw browser.
3. Prove it is real: show the live app on a device, a real person doing the flow, and the real API calls on screen (Aloud's "Wired end to end, not wrapped"); anchor with one hard stat (14.3%, 8.5M, 69 points).
4. Finish with a 8-10 s end card: tagline, QR codes (live app / install / source) and readable URLs, as Aloud did; avoid ShadeSpan's 1-2 s card and ToneGrid's no-CTA cut-off. Put the same links in the YouTube description with chapter timestamps (ShadeSpan).
5. Keep length 2:20-3:00 with clear chapter beats (problem, why unsolved, solution, live demo, close); ConfidFit's 0:50 is clean but did not win, and no winner used a long unscripted screen recording. Show the YouCam API credit early (ConfidFit's badge at 0-4 s).
